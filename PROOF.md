# Four states, one house size: quota and population monotonicity are incompatible

## Statement

A *solution* gives every population vector p ∈ ℤ>0⁴ and house size h an allocation a = f(p,h) ∈ ℕ⁴ with Σa = h.

- **Quota.** Each a_i is ⌊q_i⌋ or ⌈q_i⌉, where q_i = h·p_i / Σp.
- **Population monotonicity at a fixed house size (PM).** Take p, p′ with the same h, and states i ≠ j with
  p′_i ≥ p_i and p′_j ≤ p_j. Then it never happens that i loses a seat while j gains one.

**Theorem.** No solution satisfies quota and PM. Already the single house size h = 24 is impossible.

## The gadget

Take the 60 population vectors that are relabelings of five shapes, all at h = 24:

| shape | Σp | quotas | seats rounded up |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1,1,3,26) | 31 | 0.774, 0.774, 2.323, 20.129 | 2 |
| (1,2,2,26) | 31 | 0.774, 1.548, 1.548, 20.129 | 2 |
| (2,2,3,26) | 33 | 1.455, 1.455, 2.182, 18.909 | 2 |
| (1,3,3,26) | 33 | 0.727, 2.182, 2.182, 18.909 | 2 |
| (1,1,3,34) | 39 | 0.615, 0.615, 1.846, 20.923 | 3 |

No quota is an integer. So each vector has C(4,2) = 6 or C(4,3) = 4 quota allocations, and a solution must
choose one per vector. The claim is that no choice for all 60 vectors avoids every population paradox between
them.

**What PM says on the gadget.** Two vectors constrain each other when one state weakly grew and another weakly
shrank. The workhorse is the case where both populations are unchanged (Varshney's Lemma 1, "unchanged-state
transfer"): between two vectors with p_i = p′_i and p_j = p′_j, no seat may move from i to j.

**A sample forced rounding** (the full refutation chains many steps of this kind). In (1,1,3,26), states 3 and 4
cannot both be rounded up.
- If they were, the allocation would be (0,0,3,21).
- Compare (1,1,3,34), where only state 4 grew. Its floors are (0,0,1,20) and three of the four states round up,
  so state 3 gets at most 2 seats there: it loses a seat.
- At least one of states 1 and 2 must round up in (1,1,3,34), so that state gains a seat.
- States 1, 2 and 3 have unchanged populations, so this is a forbidden transfer.

## How the contradiction is certified

The 60 choices are encoded as 240 Boolean variables, "state i of vector P is rounded up". Quota and PM give
3,576 clauses. CaDiCaL refutes them and emits an LRAT proof: 2,021 steps using 1,796 of the clauses.

In `Apportion4.lean` (generated by `genlean.jl`):
- each of those 1,796 clauses is a separate lemma, proved from Quota and PM by `omega` on concrete numbers;
- Mathlib's `lrat_proof` replays the LRAT refutation in the Lean kernel (no native code);
- the main theorem `Apportion4.no_quota_popmono_fixed_house` combines the two.

`#print axioms` gives `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.

## Why there is no short hand proof (what was tried)

- **The gadget is tight.**
  - Removing any one of the 60 vectors makes the system satisfiable, and so does removing any one shape.
  - Keeping only the 45 vectors whose large state is not state 1 is also satisfiable.
- **No central case split.** Even after full arc consistency, most vectors keep 5–6 possible allocations, and no
  single vector has every option refuted by propagation alone. Case analysis with propagation (DPLL with forward
  checking) explores about 385,000 nodes.
- **No averaging argument.** The LP relaxation of the clauses is feasible, so no positive combination of the
  constraints yields a contradiction.
- **Why every "natural" rule fails.** Any rule that hands out seats one at a time by a score depending only on a
  state's own population and seat count (nondecreasing in population, decreasing in seats) is automatically PM.
  That includes all divisor methods and their state-specific and non-homogeneous generalizations. But z3 shows
  that no such rule satisfies quota at h = 12, already on the family "one state with population ≤ 60, the other
  three in {1,2,3}" (6,237 vectors). So a solution would have to break the global "price" order of a divisor
  method, and the 60-vector gadget shows that even this is impossible.
- **Smaller house sizes.**
  - h = 23 is also impossible (8 shapes, 104 vectors; SAT-checked only).
  - The family "one state with population ≤ 5h, the other three in {1,2,3}" is satisfiable at h = 12, 16 and 20,
    and so are all vectors in [1..6]⁴ for 2 ≤ h ≤ 7.
  - Whether some h ≤ 22 is already impossible is open; searches there were inconclusive.

In short: the obstruction is genuine but combinatorially intricate. The smallest form found is "these 60
vectors" plus a machine-checked refutation.
